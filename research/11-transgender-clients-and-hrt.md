# Training transgender and gender-diverse clients, including athletes on HRT

Research slice 11. Written 2026-08-31.

Sibling slices: 07 baselining, 08 goal-specific programming, 09 sport-specific,
10 medical and special populations, 12 trainer practice and ethics. This file
covers only trans and gender-diverse clients. Where a topic is general (bone
loading, hypertrophy dose, VTE screening) it defers to those slices.

The user's framing: "clients that have extremely specific needs, like a
male-to-female transgender individual that is still trying to maintain the
ability to function in their sport, while still feminizing their body."

Policies move fast. Every policy row below is date-stamped. The agent must
re-check any policy before a client acts on it.

## TL;DR

1. Feminizing HRT drops hemoglobin fast and muscle slowly. Hgb reaches cis-female range by about 4 months. Strength and lean mass fall roughly 5% in year one and stay above cis-female values at 36 months.
2. So endurance falls first and hardest. Measured running times in trans women athletes got about 15% slower after HRT; swim times about 5% slower.
3. Strength is the cheapest thing to defend. Wiik found trans women kept knee extension and flexion strength across 12 months while thigh muscle volume fell 5%. Keep lifting and you keep most of it.
4. Training volume predicted who lost less. In Harper 2025 the runners who raised training volume lost the least performance (r=0.719, p=0.008). That is the single most actionable finding in this file.
5. Fat redistributes to hips and legs on estrogen within one year: +42% leg fat, +34% gynoid fat, +18% android fat, hip circumference +3.2 cm. The body does the feminizing. Training does not have to.
6. There is no evidence that glute-focused training changes fat distribution. Glute hypertrophy is real and adds shape. Spot fat reduction is not.
7. Masculinizing HRT is the mirror image and it is faster: +15% thigh muscle volume, +15% quadriceps CSA, strength gains, hematocrit rising into male range within a year.
8. Bone gets better, not worse, in year one on both directions. Lumbar spine BMD +3.7% in trans women, total hip +1.0% in both. Keep loading anyway.
9. Real safety items: VTE risk on estrogen (about 2.3 per 1000 person-years pooled), post-surgical timelines, binding during exercise, and the hemoglobin drop being mistaken for overtraining.
10. Elite sport eligibility has closed sharply. The IOC adopted a policy on 26 March 2026 limiting female-category Olympic events to athletes who screen SRY-negative. Recreational sport is explicitly out of scope of that policy.
11. Most clients are not elite. Their real barriers are gym safety and body image, not eligibility rules.
12. Body-composition tracking is a dysphoria hazard. Default to performance metrics and let the client opt into anything else.

## 1. Feminizing HRT: what changes and when

Typical regimen is estradiol plus an antiandrogen (spironolactone, cyproterone
acetate, or a GnRH analogue), or estradiol alone after orchiectomy.

### 1.2 What persists

At the cross-sectional end, trans women who had been on HRT a long time still
sit between cis women and cis men on several measures.

- Alvares 2022 studied 15 trans women on HRT for 14.4 ± 3.5 years. VO2peak was 2606 ± 417 mL/min versus 2167 ± 409 in cis women and 3358 ± 436 in cis men. Handgrip was 35.3 ± 5.4 kg versus 29.7 ± 3.6 and 48.4 ± 6.7 kg. [doi:10.1136/bjsports-2021-105400](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105400)
- Hamilton 2024 studied trans athletes rather than non-athletes and found a mixed picture. Trans women had higher absolute handgrip than cis women (40.7 ± 6.8 vs 34.2 ± 3.7 kg, p=0.01) but *lower* relative jump height (0.7 ± 0.2 vs 1.0 ± 0.2 cm/kg, p<0.001) and lower relative VO2max (45.1 ± 13.3 vs 54.1 ± 6.0 mL/kg/min, p<0.001). [doi:10.1136/bjsports-2023-108029](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-108029)
- The Hamilton result is the one an agent should internalize for programming. Absolute grip strength holds up. Power relative to bodyweight and aerobic capacity relative to bodyweight both drop, because bodyweight and fat mass go up while the oxygen-carrying capacity goes down.

Note the interpretation split in the literature. Harper, Hamilton and
Pitsiladis read this evidence as arguing against blanket bans. Hilton, Lundberg
and co-authors read the same evidence as showing retained advantage
([doi:10.1007/s40279-020-01389-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-020-01389-3),
[doi:10.1111/sms.14581](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14581)). That dispute is
about sport policy, not about training. The measured numbers above are not in
dispute and are all a trainer needs.

### 2.2 What this means for a trans man in training

Year one on testosterone is the best training window most trans men will ever
get. Muscle, strength and hematocrit all move in the direction the training is
pushing. Hamilton 2024 found trans men athletes still below cis men on absolute
grip (38.8 ± 7.5 vs 45.7 ± 6.9 kg, p=0.03) and absolute VO2max (3635 ± 644 vs
4467 ± 641 mL/min, p=0.002), so the gap does not fully close in a cohort at
this training level.
[doi:10.1136/bjsports-2023-108029](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-108029)

The practical risk is doing too much because everything feels easy. Connective
tissue does not adapt at the speed muscle does. That is a general
overuse-injury caution, not a trans-specific finding.

## 3. Programming

### 3.1 The core insight for the user's example

A trans woman who wants to keep performing in her sport while feminizing is
asking two questions that mostly do not conflict.

- The feminizing part is done by hormones. Fat moves to hips and legs on its own (+42% leg, +34% gynoid, hip +3.2 cm at 12 months, Klaver 2018). Nothing in the training program causes or prevents that.
- The performance part is where training earns its keep. Harper 2025 found that the trans women runners who increased training volume lost the least performance (r=0.719, p=0.008), and that after adjusting for training, age-graded scores pre and post HRT showed no statistical difference. [doi:10.1002/ejsc.70036](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsc.70036)

So the honest message to that client is: your body will feminize regardless of
what you lift, and your best defense of sport performance is not cutting
training back.

Caveat on Harper 2025: n=10, mixed retrospective and prospective, self-selected
athletes. Treat the volume correlation as encouraging, not proven.

### 3.3 Where the evidence runs out

These questions have no trans-specific answer and the agent must say so:

- Does recovery capacity change under falling testosterone? No published study measured recovery, soreness, or session-to-session readiness across HRT. Unknown.
- Does volume need to go up to hold hypertrophy under low testosterone? No study tested it. The Harper 2025 volume-performance correlation is about endurance sport, not hypertrophy.
- Optimal protein intake under HRT? No trans-specific data.
- Regional hypertrophy targeting to shape a feminine or masculine silhouette? Muscle can be grown regionally. There is no evidence that training changes where fat goes.
- Injury risk during transition? Not studied.
- Timing training around estradiol injection cycles or testosterone injection troughs? Not studied. UNVERIFIED for any claim in either direction.

## 4. Safety and medical

### 4.1 Venous thromboembolism on estrogen

- Pooled VTE incidence in trans women on estrogen: 2.3 per 1000 person-years (95% CI 0.8 to 6.9), with high heterogeneity (I² = 74%, p=0.0039). The authors say the pooled estimate cannot be reliably applied to trans women as a group. Khan 2019, [doi:10.1373/clinchem.2018.288316](https://doi.org/10.1373/clinchem.2018.288316)
- Kaiser Permanente cohort, 2842 transfeminine and 2118 transmasculine members: transfeminine participants had higher VTE incidence, with 2-year and 8-year risk differences of 4.1 (95% CI 1.6 to 6.7) and 16.7 (95% CI 6.4 to 27.5) per 1000 persons relative to cisgender men. Differences were more pronounced in those who started hormones during follow-up. Evidence was insufficient for transmasculine participants. Getahun 2018, [doi:10.7326/M17-2785](https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-2785)
- Practical reading: absolute risk is low but not zero, and it is highest early. Long-haul travel to competitions, prolonged immobility, and recent surgery are the situations that stack with it. Standard travel advice applies (move, hydrate, compression if a clinician has advised it).
- No study has tested whether exercise raises or lowers VTE risk in this population. UNVERIFIED.

### 4.2 Antiandrogens and blood pressure

- Spironolactone is a potassium-sparing diuretic and an antiandrogen. In a comparison of 114 transfeminine people, the spironolactone group had higher serum urea than the cyproterone group but no significant difference in blood pressure, serum potassium or creatinine. Angus 2019, [doi:10.1530/EC-19-0272](https://doi.org/10.1530/EC-19-0272)
- Cyproterone acetate achieved lower testosterone (median 0.8 nmol/L, IQR 0.6 to 1.20) than spironolactone (2.0 nmol/L, IQR 0.9 to 9.4, p=0.037). Same source.
- Practical reading for a trainer: spironolactone's diuretic effect makes hydration and heat management worth asking about, and dizziness on standing is worth flagging. Any client on spironolactone reporting dizziness, cramping, or palpitations goes to their prescriber, not to a program change. The agent never comments on dosing or drug choice.

Rule for the agent: if the client has had or is scheduled for surgery, ask for
the surgeon's written restrictions and program to those. Never generate a
timeline.

### 4.4 Binding during exercise

- Peitzmeier 2017 surveyed 1800 adults assigned female at birth with binding experience. 51.5% bound daily. Over 97% reported at least one of 28 negative outcomes attributed to binding. Frequency in days per week was associated with negative outcomes in 22 of 28 outcomes. Compression methods associated with symptoms were commercial binders (20 of 28 outcomes), elastic bandages (14 of 28) and duct tape or plastic wrap (13 of 28). Larger chest size was mainly associated with dermatological problems. [doi:10.1080/13691058.2016.1191675](https://doi.org/10.1080/13691058.2016.1191675)
- The study did not measure exercise specifically, and did not measure lung function objectively. It is self-reported outcomes. So "binding restricts breathing during exercise" is plausible and widely reported but not demonstrated by instrumented study. Flag it as such.
- What the agent can safely say: binding frequency drives symptoms, elastic bandages and tape are the worst options, and the authors' own conclusion is that clinicians should assess binding practice and help manage risk rather than tell people to stop.
- What the agent should not do: tell a client to stop binding. That is a dysphoria-loaded instruction and not the trainer's call. Offer options instead. Ask whether a sports bra or a looser binder is possible for the session, keep a change of top available, and program shorter work intervals with longer rests if the client says breathing is limited.
- Tucking during exercise: I found no peer-reviewed study of tucking and exercise. UNVERIFIED. Practical concerns raised in community and clinical guidance (heat, chafing, discomfort) have no research base I can cite.

### 4.5 Hemoglobin and how it fools a trainer

This is the most likely misread in the whole file. A trans woman four to six
months into HRT will have measurably lower oxygen-carrying capacity (Hgb 153 to
135 g/L in Harper 2025's cohort). Her easy pace is genuinely slower. Her heart
rate at a given pace is genuinely higher.

An agent that has not accounted for this will read the pattern as overtraining,
under-recovery, illness, or poor adherence, and will deload a client who does
not need deloading. Explicitly check for HRT status and time-on-HRT before
attributing an endurance drop to fatigue.

Mirror case: a trans man's rising hematocrit means his lab values should be
read against male reference ranges, not female (UCSF, Deutsch 2016). That is a
clinician's job, not the agent's, but the agent should not panic at a "high"
hematocrit reading it against the wrong range.

### 4.6 Refer-to-clinician triggers

Send to a clinician, do not adjust the program:

- Any new unilateral calf pain, swelling, warmth, chest pain, or shortness of breath in a client on estrogen. Possible VTE or PE. Urgent.
- Sudden severe headache, visual change, one-sided weakness or speech change. Getahun 2018 found more pronounced ischemic stroke differences in transfeminine participants who started hormones during follow-up. Emergency.
- Dizziness on standing, muscle cramps, palpitations, or unusual thirst in a client on spironolactone.
- Any request to change hormone dose or timing, for any reason including training. Always the prescriber.
- Chest pain, rib pain, skin breakdown, numbness, or shortness of breath associated with binding.
- Any post-surgical wound change: increased pain, redness, discharge, opening.
- Reported disordered eating, food restriction to reach a body shape, or compulsive exercise. Teti 2020 found trans masculine young people used restricted eating to reach a target male shape when they could not exercise. [doi:10.1080/26895269.2020.1719950](https://doi.org/10.1080/26895269.2020.1719950)
- Self-harm or suicidality disclosed in any context. Immediate handoff per slice 12.
- Fatigue or performance drop that does not fit the expected HRT timeline, especially if the client is not on HRT or is years into it.

### 5.1 How policy affects programming

Mostly it does not, and saying so plainly is a kindness.

- If the client competes recreationally, none of the above applies to them. Say that.
- If the client competes under a federation, the eligibility question is settled by paperwork and screening, not by their training. The agent's role is to name the governing body, link the rule, and suggest they contact the federation directly.
- Under the older testosterone-threshold rules (still live in some federations), a client may need documented serum testosterone below a threshold for a defined period. That is a clinician and federation matter. The agent never advises on achieving a testosterone number.
- The agent never predicts whether a client will be found eligible.
- The agent never editorializes about whether a policy is right.

## 6. Practice, intake and wording

### 6.1 The barrier is the gym, not the physiology

- Lightner 2024 systematic review of physical activity among transgender individuals: rates of physical activity are lower than in cisgender or sexual-minority comparison groups, and trans women were less likely to engage in physical activity than other groups. Qualitative findings named stigma, discrimination, body image, unwelcoming environments (gyms, locker rooms, swimming pools) and the dichotomous structure of sport as contributors. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0297571](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0297571)
- Teti 2020, interviews with 16 trans masculine young adults: they wanted to exercise to reach a body shape, not for health or stress relief. They found gyms unwelcoming and feared discrimination. When they could not exercise, some used restricted eating to reach the shape they wanted. [doi:10.1080/26895269.2020.1719950](https://doi.org/10.1080/26895269.2020.1719950)
- Programming implication: home and equipment-light options are not a fallback for this population, they are often the primary route. Ask about environment before assuming gym access.
- Screening implication: shape-driven motivation plus inability to train is a documented pathway to restriction. Ask about eating when a client reports a training gap.

### 6.2 WPATH SOC8

Standards of Care Version 8, Coleman et al. 2022, Int J Transgend Health
23(sup1):S1-S259,
[doi:10.1080/26895269.2022.2100644](https://doi.org/10.1080/26895269.2022.2100644).
It is the reference document for gender-affirming care and covers assessment,
hormone therapy, surgery, primary care and institutional environments. It is
not a training document and contains no exercise prescription. Cite it for
care-context questions, not for programming.

### 6.3 Intake additions

Add to the slice 07 intake. Keep it short and make everything optional.

Ask always:
- Name to use, and pronouns. Store both. Use them everywhere including in generated program documents.
- "Is there a different name on any medical or account records I should expect to see?" Only if the system will surface records.

Ask because programming genuinely needs it:
- "Are you on any hormone therapy? If so, which direction, and roughly when did you start?" Drives the whole adjustments table.
- "Any surgery, past or planned, with activity restrictions?" Drives section 4.3.
- "Do you bind, tuck, or wear compression when you train?" Drives 4.4 and session design.
- "Any body areas you would rather I did not measure, photograph, or talk about?" Drives 6.4.
- "Changing rooms and gym environment: anything I should design around?" Drives 6.1.

Never ask:
- Surgical status when no surgery restriction is in play.
- Birth name, unless a record system forces it.
- Genital anatomy, ever.
- "How long have you known?" or anything about the client's identity history.
- Anything that is curiosity rather than programming input.

The rule: every question must trace to a line in the program. If it does not,
it is not intake, it is intrusion.

### 6.4 Body-composition tracking and dysphoria

Body-composition metrics are the highest-risk part of a fitness agent for this
population. Two things collide. Fat distribution is deliberately changing, so
the numbers move for reasons that have nothing to do with training. And the
numbers themselves are gendered.

Design rules:

- Default to performance metrics. Load lifted, reps, session pace, work completed, consistency. These are unambiguous and they are what the client controls.
- Never volunteer weight, BMI, body-fat percentage, or waist-to-hip ratio. Offer them as an opt-in, once, and never bring them up again if declined.
- If body composition is tracked, ask which measurements the client wants. Hip circumference may be affirming to one client and distressing to another. Chest circumference is high-risk for both directions.
- Never use gendered normative reference ranges without saying which range and why. "Above average for men your age" is a sentence that can end a client relationship.
- Never frame a hormonal change as a training failure. Lean mass falling 5% in a trans woman's first year is the treatment working, not the program failing. Say that out loud in the progress report.
- Never frame a hormonal change as a training success either. Muscle gained in a trans man's first year on testosterone is partly the hormone. Attributing it all to the program sets up a crash when the rate slows.

### 6.5 Wording

| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| "trans woman", "trans man", "trans and gender-diverse people" | "transgendered", "a transgender", "transsexual" unless the client uses it |
| "assigned male at birth" / "assigned female at birth", and only when clinically needed | "biologically male", "born a man", "natal sex" |
| "gender-affirming hormone therapy" or "HRT" | "cross-sex hormones", "sex change hormones" |
| "cisgender women", when a comparison is actually needed | "normal women", "real women", "women" as the contrast to "trans women" |
| "your hormone therapy" | "your transition" as a catch-all |
| "your surgeon's restrictions" | any generic post-op timeline |
| "the federation's current rule is X, here is the link" | any opinion on whether the rule is fair |

Additional wording rules for a generating agent:

- Mirror the client's own vocabulary for their body and their transition. If they say "chest", say chest. If they say "top surgery", say top surgery.
- Do not compliment feminization or masculinization progress unprompted. It reads as surveillance.
- Do not use gendered encouragement ("beast mode", "strong girl") unless the client has used it first.
- Program names and exercise names should not be gendered. No "glutes and abs for her".
- If the client raises dysphoria, acknowledge it and adapt the session. Do not counsel. Slice 12 owns the boundary.

## 7. Non-binary and non-HRT clients

There is no separate physiology here and pretending there is would be
condescending. Someone not on hormone therapy has the hormonal profile they
have. Program from goals and baseline, exactly as slice 08 says.

What is trans-specific is the goal vocabulary. Clients often describe wanting a
shape rather than a number.

- Broader shoulders and a narrower-looking waist: lateral deltoid and upper back volume, plus general leanness. Waist width is skeletal and fat-driven, not trainable. Say so.
- Fuller hips and glutes: glute hypertrophy work. Effective for muscle, not for fat placement.
- Less upper-body bulk: reduce upper-body hypertrophy volume while keeping enough loading for joint and spine health. Note that muscle already built does not disappear quickly.
- Flatter chest appearance without surgery: no training intervention achieves this. Say so plainly rather than selling chest exercises.
- Androgynous or ambiguous shape: often means avoiding the emphasis that ordinary programs default to. Ask which regions they want de-emphasized and just do less there.

All of the above is cis-derived hypertrophy evidence applied to a different
goal. There is no trans-specific study of aesthetic programming. Say that when
the client asks how confident you are.

## 8. Evidence quality

### Solid
- Hemoglobin falls to cis-female range within about 4 months on feminizing HRT. Consistent across the systematic review and measured directly in an athlete cohort. (Harper 2021, Harper 2025)
- Regional fat redistribution direction and rough magnitude at 12 months. Large multicenter prospective DXA study, 179 trans women and 162 trans men. (Klaver 2018)
- Muscle volume and CSA changes at 12 months, roughly -5% feminizing and +15% masculinizing. Direct imaging. (Wiik 2020)
- Bone mineral density rises in year one on both directions. 231 trans women and 199 trans men, prospective multicenter DXA. (Wiepjes 2017)
- Strength and lean mass remain above cis-female values through 36 months of feminizing HRT. Multiple independent lines. (Harper 2021, Hilton & Lundberg 2021, Roberts 2020, Alvares 2022)

### Moderate
- Field-performance change magnitudes. Roberts 2020 is large (n=75) but uses military fitness tests, not sport. Harper 2025 uses real competition results but n=10.
- Pooled body-composition meta-analysis. GRADE certainty was moderate for BMI and low to very low for lean mass, fat mass and waist circumference. (Gois 2025)
- VTE incidence. Pooled but heterogeneous (I²=74%), and the authors say the estimate should not be applied to the group as a whole. (Khan 2019)
- Binding harms. Large sample (n=1800) but self-reported, cross-sectional, non-random, and no objective respiratory measurement. (Peitzmeier 2017)

### Thin or absent
- Recovery, soreness, and readiness under HRT. No study.
- Volume or intensity prescriptions specific to trans clients. No study.
- Protein or nutrition targets specific to HRT. No study.
- Injury incidence during transition. No study.
- Training timed around hormone administration cycles. No study.
- Return-to-exercise protocols after gender-affirming surgery. No peer-reviewed protocol located. Surgeon practice guidance only.
- Tucking and exercise. No study located.
- Long-term bone health beyond a few years on HRT. Thin.

### Extrapolated from cis populations
Everything in section 3.2 that is not directly cited to a trans study:
hypertrophy dose-response, tendon adaptation lag, glute hypertrophy
programming, upper-body volume manipulation, bone loading recommendations. The
agent should label these as general exercise science applied to a trans client,
not as trans-specific evidence, whenever a client asks how confident it is.

### Sources I was asked for and could not verify
- "Yun et al. 2023 meta-analysis". I searched PubMed by author, topic and year and found nothing matching. I substituted Gois et al. 2025 (Rev Endocr Metab Disord), a systematic review and meta-analysis of BMI and body composition changes on GAHT, [doi:10.1007/s11154-025-09985-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11154-025-09985-2). If the user has a specific Yun paper in mind, please supply the DOI.
- Endocrine Society 2017 guideline expected-change tables. The guideline itself is verified (Hembree et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2017;102(11):3869-3903, [doi:10.1210/jc.2017-01658](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2017-01658)) but I could not retrieve the specific onset/maximum-effect tables. I did not reproduce them from memory. The timeline tables above use measured study data instead, which is stronger anyway.
- "Lundberg/Hamilton 2024 update papers". These are largely correspondence and commentary rather than new data. I cited Lundberg 2024 (Scand J Med Sci Sports, [doi:10.1111/sms.14581](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14581)) and Hilton & Lundberg's 2024 reply ([doi:10.1007/s40279-023-01930-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01930-0)) as context for the interpretation split, not as evidence.

I cannot ask directly, so recording them here.

1. The Yun 2023 meta-analysis. Do you have the DOI? I could not find it and substituted Gois 2025.
2. How much should the agent volunteer about sport eligibility policy? Options: never mention it unless asked; mention once at intake if the client says they compete; always surface it for competitive clients. This is a product decision with real consequences and I did not want to pick it for you.
3. Should body-composition tracking be off by default for all clients, or off by default only for trans clients? Off-for-all is simpler and arguably better for everyone, but it changes slice 07's baselining design.
4. Do you want a policy-refresh mechanism? Every policy row in section 5 will be stale within a year. Options: a dated "last checked" field the agent surfaces; a scheduled re-research task; or the agent always fetching live before answering a policy question.

6. How hard should the agent push on the disordered-eating screen? Teti 2020 documents a real pathway, but repeated screening questions can feel like surveillance to someone already scrutinized.
7. Does the agent ever hold hormone-therapy details, or only a coarse flag (direction plus start date)? Storing less is safer for a population with real safety risks around data. The programming table only needs direction and time-on-HRT.
