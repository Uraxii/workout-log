## Job definition (verbatim)

"The ACSM Certified Personal Trainer (ACSM-CPT) is qualified to plan and implement exercise programs for healthy individuals or those who have medical clearance to exercise. The ACSM-CPT facilitates motivation and adherence, as well as develops and administers programs designed to enhance muscular strength, endurance, flexibility, cardiorespiratory fitness, body composition and/or any of the motor skill-related components of physical fitness."

## The domain that governs scope and referral (verbatim)

Domain IV, "Legal and Professional Responsibilities" (10% of the exam), task A:

"Collaborate with health care professionals, allied health practitioners, and organizations within their respective scope of practice to optimize the advantages and mitigate the potential drawbacks of an exercise prescription."

Knowledge required under that task (verbatim): "reputable professional resources and referral sources to ensure client safety and program effectiveness"; "the scope of practice for the Certified Personal Trainer and the need to practice within this scope"; "effective and professional communication with allied health and fitness professionals"; "documenting exercise sessions and performing periodic re-evaluations to assess changes in fitness status."

Skills required (verbatim): "effective communication skills to interact with healthcare professionals and allied health practitioners"; "assessing individual needs and modifying exercise programs to take into account client history and potential contraindications"; "documenting and sharing relevant information with other members of the healthcare team"; "adapting and modifying exercise programs based on feedback and input from other members of the healthcare team."

## Emergency action plan as a named, required artifact (verbatim)

Domain IV, task B: "Implement a comprehensive risk management program (including an emergency action plan and injury prevention program) consistent with industry standards of care," requiring knowledge of "resources available to obtain basic life support, automated external defibrillator (AED) and cardiopulmonary resuscitation certification" and "the need for, and components of, an emergency action plan."

## A worked example distinguishing in-scope judgment from a required referral (verbatim, from the outline's own sample exam question)

"During the preparticipation screening, a client presents with the following information: Physical activity: jogs 2-3 times per week at 70% of HRR for 20 minutes; Current medication: high blood pressure medication; Health history: A physical examination was conducted 10 months ago, family physician cleared the client to exercise at that time; Goal: Participate in a marathon for the first time in six months. The client would like to begin an exercise program right away." The outline's answer key treats "Discontinue the screening and request a current exercise clearance" as the correct synthesis-level response, i.e., referring back to the physician rather than proceeding.
