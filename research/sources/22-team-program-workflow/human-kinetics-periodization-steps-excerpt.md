## Extracted content

The planning process involves seven interrelated steps, with the strength and conditioning professional named as the primary planner, who evaluates previous performance and consults with athletes.

Building the annual plan around competition: the competition calendar drives the planning structure. Coaches must first "place all planned competitions and their locations on the planning sheet," then prioritize them to "determine where peak performance is needed."

Hierarchical structure, in descending order:

1. Multiyear plan - establishes long-term athlete development (example given: high school freshman through senior years).
2. Annual plan - created during the transition phase at year's end.
3. Macrocycles - determined after competitions are prioritized; typically contain phases of roughly 5-12 weeks.
4. Phases - divided into preparatory, competitive, and transitional phases.
5. Mesocycles - subdivisions within macrocycles for sequencing training factors.
6. Microcycles - individual week plans, considering the athlete's daily schedule and need for recovery days.
7. Individual sessions - specific workout design.

A "peaking index" (a 1-5 scale) is described as a tool to indicate readiness levels through the annual framework.
