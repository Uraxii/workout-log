## Who sets the calendar the team plans around

The World Rugby Sevens Series (WRSS) competition schedule is set externally by World Rugby. The French national team's coaching staff then built their periodization around that external calendar plus the fixed timing of the Rio Olympic Games.

## The season's structure, built backward from the fixed events

The plan covered a 48-week period across the 2015-2016 Olympic season, divided into three macrocycles: preseason (12 weeks), in-season (25 weeks), and Olympic preparation (11 weeks). Per the extraction, the source states: "The Olympics are held ~11 weeks after the WRSS final tournament and as such players will probably follow different preparation and load management regimens across the season."

## Monitoring artifact and what it fed

External training load (TL) was tracked continuously using GPS devices in both training and competition, alongside RPE. Per the extraction: "External TL was monitored in training and competition over the entire duration of the season using a global positioning system (GPS) devices."

## Revision cadence and trigger

Plan adjustments are reported at mesocycle intervals of roughly 4-7 weeks. The coaching staff reduced workload mid-season in response to observed fatigue patterns and contextual factors (for example the timing of qualification tournaments), which the extraction characterizes as "responsive planning rather than static periodization."
