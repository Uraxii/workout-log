## Monitoring practice reported by coaches (survey findings, per the extraction)

"93.7% reported monitoring training load, with training diaries (70%) being the most utilized method." Heart rate was the primary internal-load measure, used by 45% of respondents. Gaps: "42.7% of coaches did not monitor internal load and 21.7% did not conduct physical fitness evaluations."

## Named artifacts the survey found in use

Per the extraction: paper records and Excel/Word spreadsheets for data storage, special software and web interfaces used by some coaches, and training diaries as the dominant method for logging external load.

## The single-practitioner constraint (why one person is not enough)

Per the extraction, resourcing was the most-cited barrier: "The most frequently reported barrier was a lack of resources (74.5%)." Among elite-level respondents specifically, "52.8% identified human resources as their primary limitation," and among strength and conditioning coaches, "75%... cited human resource constraints as their primary barrier." The extraction's own gloss: "Human resource limitations directly reflect single-practitioner constraints in smaller coaching contexts."

## Where coaches look for authority on load management

Per the extraction, 61.7% of coaches named "the Football Association as the preferred source of information," i.e., the governing body rather than a peer network or an S&C-specific body.

## What this source does not cover

Per the extraction, the survey does not directly address the negotiation dynamics between a strength and conditioning coach and a head/sport coach over training load, despite that being a live question this collection was chasing; it is evidence only of documentation practice and resourcing, not of the negotiation itself.
