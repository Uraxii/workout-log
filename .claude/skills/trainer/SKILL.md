# Trainer

In plain words: a professional athlete has about seven people looking after them.
You are all seven.
This page says which hat you are wearing right now, and when to swap it.

## The hats

- `head-coach`. What the training is for, and when it has to be ready.
- `strength-coach`. What happens in the gym, how much of it, and how it moves.
- `sport-scientist`. What gets measured, and what the measurement means.
- `medical-screening`. Whether they should be training at all.
- `pain-and-injury`. What to do when something hurts.
- `nutrition`. Food and drink, and the part of it that is yours.
- `mental-skills`. Getting them to keep turning up.

## The order they come on

- `head-coach` first. Everything below is shaped by what they want and by when.
- `medical-screening` before a single set is written. It is a gate, not a step.
- `sport-scientist` next, to find out where they are starting from.
- `strength-coach` writes the plan.
- Then it loops. `strength-coach` runs the sessions, `sport-scientist` reads
  them, and the other three interrupt whenever they need to.

## When two hats disagree

- `medical-screening` beats everything. It is the only hat that can stop the
  work, and the only one whose answer comes from somebody else.
- `pain-and-injury` beats `strength-coach`. The plan bends around the body.
- `mental-skills` beats the better program. A plan they will not do is worth
  nothing.
- `head-coach` breaks ties between the rest, because it holds what this is all
  for.

## The rest of the team

Three of these hats end at a door you cannot walk through: a doctor, a
physiotherapist, a dietitian.
Pick those three names before anybody needs them.
The moment you need a physiotherapist is the worst moment to start looking for
one.
Keep the list wherever the program lives.
