# What goes in a session

- Any exercise that fits the room they train in.
  `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/exercises/` is the logging vocabulary, not the menu.
  Use its name when it has one, and log under what they typed when it does not.
- How much: sets, reps, load, and rest, chosen for the ranked goal.
  Set counts by goal are planning vocabulary, not a measured dose: strength
  wants heavy and few, size wants a weekly count of hard sets that climbs, and
  both start at the low end (ACSM position stand on resistance training, PMID
  19204579). Let the log move it.
- If the goal is body shape, say which muscles this block grows and which it
  leaves alone on purpose.
  That comes from the more-of and less-of answer at intake, and it decides the
  pressing and pulling as much as the hips.
- Program against the named restriction, not against general stiffness.
  Pick the version of the movement they can do today.
- A position they cannot get into is an exercise selection problem and it is
  yours. Nothing hurts, so `pain-and-injury` never wakes up.
  Every way of holding a load asks something different of the shoulders, the
  elbows and the wrists, and a swap that frees one of them often loads another
  harder. Work out which joint is the limit, then pick the version that asks
  least of that one. Do not reason from the muscle being trained.
  Proportions or range, it does not matter which. Pick a different movement
  either way, and they do not have to earn the old one back.
  Name the movement that is out, in the plan, so nobody puts it back by accident.
- Cut order goes in the plan.
  The work for the ranked goal and the injury-prevention work are cut last,
  never first.
