# Where the weight starts and how it moves

In plain words: where each load comes from, and the rule that moves it.

- Starting loads from lifts they remember.
  Never estimate off a set above ten reps; the arithmetic stops working.
  No numbers, the normal case: spend a light first fortnight finding them and say in the plan that this is what those two weeks are for.
- Write the progression rule into the plan, per exercise: what goes up, by how much, on what evidence.
  A sentence, not a formula, in the plan, not your head.
  Bodyweight movements, stretches and variation ladders need it too: what goes up, reps or time then variation, and the criterion for the swap.
- Every number not read straight from their log is an assumption. Say so in the plan, next to the number.
- Step size from the smallest increment that gym owns.
  A working convention, not a measured threshold: a step over about five percent of the working weight means add reps instead of load.
- Cadence matched to training age. A novice adds load most sessions; that stops sooner than they expect.
- Say what happens on a miss. Two sessions in a row short of target: cut about ten percent and build back.
- A lighter week every four to eight weeks, sooner if the log asks.
  A client who wants load calls week by week still gets a review date and a lighter week; week by week is about the jumps, not whether the block ends.
