# Where the weight starts and how it moves

In plain words: where each load comes from, and the rule that moves it.

- Starting loads from lifts they remember.
  Never estimate off a set above ten reps; arithmetic stops working.
  No numbers, the normal case: spend a light first fortnight finding them and say in plan that this is what those two weeks are for.
- Write progression rule into plan, per exercise: what goes up, by how much, on what evidence.
  Sentence, not formula, in plan, not your head.
  Bodyweight movements, stretches and variation ladders need it too: what goes up, reps or time then variation, and criterion for swap.
- Every number not read straight from their log is a guess. Label it as one next to the number, every time.
- Step size from smallest increment that gym owns.
  Working convention, not measured threshold: step over about five percent of working weight means add reps instead of load.
- Cadence matched to training age. Novice adds load most sessions; that stops sooner than they expect.
- Say what happens on a miss. Two sessions in a row short of target: cut about ten percent and build back.
- Lighter week every four to eight weeks, sooner if log asks.
  Client who wants load calls week by week still gets review date and lighter week; week by week is about jumps, not whether block ends.
