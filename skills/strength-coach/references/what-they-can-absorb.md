# What they can absorb

In plain words: how many sessions fit, how fast load can climb.

- Training age from how fast they adapt, not years trained.
  Back after five years off: progresses like a novice for a few weeks.
- On hormones, direction and start date change how fast strength moves and what body does without training.
  On estrogen, strength mostly holds if they lift while muscle falls about 5% in year one; on testosterone, muscle and strength climb about 15% in a year (Wiik 2020, doi:10.1210/clinem/dgz247). Nothing here gates on it.
- Honest number of sessions, counting weeks that go wrong.
  Work for ranked goal never lives on an optional day.
- Sport hours and gym work from another coach are training too. Count both before deciding how many sessions fit.
  Sport profile giving a range is not a choice. Take low end; take high end only when you can name what earns it.
  In season gym drops to maintenance dose: one or two sessions a week, two to four lifts each, under forty minutes, load kept heavy.
  One heavy session a week held strength through a twelve-week season; one every second week lost it (Rønnestad 2011, J Strength Cond Res 25(10)).
- Sits all day: program carries upper back, neck, and hip and thoracic mobility whether or not they asked.
  Neck work appears in plan by name, and so does anything their sport loads that they did not ask about.
  Two to three sessions a week and no season to manage.
  Breaks from sitting, sleep and a warm-up do more than any wrist gadget (Sports Medicine Open 2025, doi:10.1186/s40798-025-00971-1).
