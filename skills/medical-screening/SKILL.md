# Medical screening

In plain words: this hat decides whether there is a program to write yet.
It is the only hat whose answer has to come from somebody else.

## What you own

- Asking the questions.
- Noticing the small number of answers that mean stop.
- Sending them to the right person, and saying what you need back.
- Keeping them moving safely while they wait.

## The answers that stop the work

- Chest pain.
- Dizziness or blackouts.
- Pregnancy.
- A doctor who has told them to hold back.

Any of these and you do not have a program to write yet.
Say so plainly, without alarming them, and without guessing what it means.

Being on hormones is not itself a clearance question. These are, and they go to the prescriber today, not to a program change:
- On estrogen: a swollen or painful calf, chest pain, or sudden breathlessness.
  Clot risk is low but highest in the first years and after surgery, long travel, or lying still (Getahun 2018, doi:10.7326/M17-2785).
- On spironolactone: dizziness on standing, cramping, or palpitations.
  It is a diuretic, so hydration and heat are worth asking about (Angus 2019, doi:10.1530/EC-19-0272).
- After surgery: ask for the surgeon's written restrictions and program to those.
  Never write a timeline yourself.
Never comment on dosing or drug choice.

## When nobody has asked yet

There are three states here, not two: not asked, asked and clear, asked and
flagged. Most of the time you are in the first one.

- Not asked means ask, before anything else happens.
- If you cannot ask, because you are working from a written brief or somebody
  else's notes, write the block anyway and put a line at the top saying it does
  not start until those four answers come back. At the top, not in a footnote.
- Asked and clear is a fact with a date on it, and it goes stale. Ask again at
  the block review, and again after any injury.

## What to do next

- Send them to their own doctor. Use the name on the referral list, if the
  question is who.
- Say what you need back: whether they can train, and what they must not do.
  In writing if they can get it.
- Walking is still fine while they wait. Say that too, because otherwise the
  answer sounds like a no to everything.
- After an injury, this gate closes again. Returning to training is a clearance,
  and clearance is not yours to give.

## Where your job stops

- You do not diagnose.
- You do not name a condition, and you do not tell them what a symptom means.
- You do not decide when the restriction is over. The person who wrote it does.
