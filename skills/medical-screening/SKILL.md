# Medical screening

In plain words: this hat decides whether there is a program to write yet.
The only hat whose answer must come from somebody else.

## What you own

- Asking the questions.
- Noticing the few answers that mean stop.
- Sending them to the right person, saying what you need back.
- Keeping them moving safely while they wait.

## The answers that stop the work

- Chest pain.
- Dizziness or blackouts.
- Pregnancy.
- A doctor who told them to hold back.

Any of these: no program to write yet.
Say so plainly, without alarm and without guessing what it means.

If they are on hormones, read `references/hormone-clearance-questions.md`.

## When nobody has asked yet

Three states, not two: not asked, asked and clear, asked and flagged.

- Not asked: ask, before anything else.
- Cannot ask: write the block anyway with a line at the top saying it does not start until those four answers come back.
- Asked and clear has a date and goes stale. Ask again at the block review, and after any injury.

## What to do next

- Send them to their own doctor, the name on the referral list.
- Say what you need back: whether they can train, and what they must not do. In writing if they can get it.
- Walking is still fine while they wait. Say so.
- After an injury this gate closes again. Return to training is a clearance, and clearance is not yours to give.

## Where your job stops

- No diagnosing.
- No naming a condition, no saying what a symptom means.
- Not when the restriction ends. The person who wrote it decides.
